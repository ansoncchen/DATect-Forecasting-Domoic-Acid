\title{OAD: An Unsupervised 3D Masked-Autoencoder for Coastal Ocean Anomaly Detection, Validated Against In-Situ ESP Biology}

\author{Anson Chen\\
Paul G.\ Allen School of Computer Science \& Engineering\\
University of Washington, Seattle, WA 98195\\

%================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Harmful algal blooms (HABs) along the U.S.\ Pacific Northwest coast are visible in satellite imagery, but per-pixel features such as chlorophyll-a have shown poor skill at predicting downstream biological targets. We present OAD, an unsupervised 3D convolutional masked-autoencoder trained on 22 years (2003--2024) of MODIS Aqua imagery over the 41--49\textdegree\,N coastal box. The model ingests four ocean channels (chl-a, Kd490, nFLH, SST) on a 321$\times$409 grid with a 4-day temporal window and produces a daily per-region scalar anomaly score from per-pixel reconstruction error. Three independent lines of evidence support the representation: at a 7-day forecast horizon OAD is the only method with positive $R^2$ in every PNW region tested (0.10--0.26) while matched-dimensionality PCA collapses to $\approx 0$; the score correlates significantly with in-situ Environmental Sample Processor measurements at the NEMO mooring during the 2016--2018 ChaBa deployments (\textit{Pseudo-nitzschia} cells $r{=}{+}0.46$, $p{=}3{\times}10^{-5}$; particulate domoic acid $r{=}{+}0.33$, $p{=}1{\times}10^{-3}$); and the 22-year inter-annual structure recovers documented events without supervision, including the 2014--16 Pacific marine heatwave. We characterize a modest cloud-cover confound (19--24\% of score variance) that downstream consumers can mitigate by carrying the in-region valid-pixel fraction as a parallel feature.
\end{abstract}

%================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Toxin-producing \textit{Pseudo-nitzschia} blooms occur annually along the U.S.\ Pacific Northwest (PNW) coast, with the resulting domoic acid (DA) contaminating razor clam shellfish and triggering recurring harvest closures \cite{Trainer2009,McCabe2023}. These events are not invisible from space: elevated chlorophyll-a, characteristic SST patterns, enhanced fluorescence, and changes in water clarity are all captured by MODIS Aqua. Yet single-channel per-pixel satellite features have historically shown poor predictive skill for downstream biological targets \cite{Anderson2016,Hill2020}. Bloom heterogeneity, species-specific toxicity, and pervasive PNW cloud cover all complicate direct supervised approaches.

We ask a different question: rather than predict a specific target from raw pixels, can an unsupervised model learn a compressed representation of \emph{ocean state} from the satellite record alone, in a way that captures bloom-relevant structure without ground-truth labels? Masked autoencoders \cite{He2022MAE} have transformed self-supervised learning of natural images by reconstructing aggressively hidden inputs, forcing the model to learn semantic structure beyond local texture. Adapting this principle to coastal ocean color---where the dominant signal is biophysical rather than land-cover categorical---is the contribution of this work.

We present \textbf{OAD} (Ocean Anomaly Detection), a 3D convolutional masked-autoencoder trained on 22 years of MODIS Aqua imagery over the PNW coast (\cref{fig:study-region}). The model produces a daily scalar anomaly score per region from per-pixel reconstruction error and is intended as a label-free representation for downstream HAB-related applications. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
    \item A 3D ConvAE with three-phase masked-autoencoder training that converts MODIS imagery into a per-region daily anomaly index.
    \item Lead-time forecastability comparison showing OAD is the only method with positive $R^2$ in every PNW region at the 7-day horizon, while matched-dimensionality PCA and climatology baselines collapse.
    \item Independent in-situ validation against Environmental Sample Processor measurements at the NEMO mooring during 2016--2018 ChaBa deployments---the score correlates significantly with both \textit{Pn} cell density ($r{=}{+}0.46$) and particulate domoic acid ($r{=}{+}0.33$) with bootstrap 95\% CIs excluding zero.
    \item Recovery of documented inter-annual events without supervision, notably the 2014--16 Pacific marine heatwave (``the Blob'') and the 2019 bloom.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Satellite remote sensing for HABs.} Operational monitoring has long used satellite chlorophyll-a as a near-real-time bloom indicator \cite{Stumpf2003,Wynne2008}, but in PNW coastal water the relationship between chl-a and beach-level toxin outcomes is weak \cite{Trainer2002,Trainer2009}. Multi-channel approaches that combine SST, fluorescence, and clarity perform incrementally better \cite{Anderson2016}, but the per-pixel single-day framing remains limiting.

\noindent\textbf{Self-supervised learning for Earth observation.} Masked autoencoders \cite{He2022MAE} train a network to reconstruct large hidden fractions of the input, forcing learning of higher-level structure. In Earth observation, SatMAE \cite{Cong2022SatMAE} and follow-up work \cite{Wang2023MAE} apply the principle to optical satellite imagery, primarily for classification. We adapt it to a 3D (time-augmented) coastal ocean cube where the temporal axis lets the model learn dynamics alongside spatial structure---a regime where the dominant signal is biophysical, not land-cover categorical.

\noindent\textbf{In-situ validation via ESP/ChaBa.} The Environmental Sample Processor \cite{Scholin2017} is an autonomous in-water sampler deployed at the NEMO mooring during ChaBa \cite{Moore2021ESP}, measuring \textit{Pn} cell density (sandwich hybridization) and particulate domoic acid (competitive ELISA) at daily cadence. These datasets are our independent biological validation target.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Data.} The training cube is built from MODIS Aqua Level-3 daily ocean-color products \cite{NASA_OceanColor} covering 2003--2024 ($\sim$4{,}700 daily rolling 8-day composites) on a 321$\times$409 grid at 0.025\textdegree\ resolution. Four channels are used: chl-a, Kd490 (water clarity), normalized fluorescence (nFLH), and SST. A 2D static ocean/land mask excludes land pixels; NaN pixels (cloud-flagged or otherwise unobserved) are excluded per-pixel from the masked-MSE reconstruction loss.

\noindent\textbf{Architecture.} OAD is a 3D ConvAE operating on tensors of shape $(C{=}4,\, T{=}4,\, H{=}321,\, W{=}409)$ (\cref{fig:architecture}). The encoder applies four blocks of 3D convolution with stride-2 spatial downsampling, GroupNorm, and GELU activation, producing a latent of dimension 32. The decoder mirrors the encoder with transposed convolutions and upsampling.

\noindent\textbf{Three-phase training.} \emph{Phase A} (plain autoencoder) produced reconstruction error that correlated poorly with bloom events---the model learned to copy textures. \emph{Phase B} added per-region z-score post-processing, which improved scale but not the underlying representation. \emph{Phase C} is masked-autoencoder pretraining \cite{He2022MAE} with 50\% random pixel masking, forcing the model to infer hidden pixels from larger spatial context. We select Phase~C with 50\% masking (\texttt{mae050}) over a 70\%-masking variant because mae050 has lower cloud confound (\cref{sec:cloud}) without sacrificing multi-step forecast skill.

\noindent\textbf{Score derivation.} For each daily composite at region $R$, the trained model produces a per-pixel reconstruction. We average per-pixel reconstruction error over in-region ocean pixels and z-score the result against an in-region historical distribution, producing a single scalar per (region, day). Downstream analyses operate only on this per-region daily scalar; the convolutional model is not invoked again at consumption time.

\centering

\caption{OAD 3D convolutional masked-autoencoder. Input is a 4-channel ($C{=}4$), 4-day-window ($T{=}4$), 321$\times$409 tensor. The encoder applies four stride-2 3D conv blocks to a latent of dimension 32; the decoder symmetrically reconstructs the input. During Phase~C training, 50\% of input pixels are randomly masked and the reconstruction loss is computed only on ocean pixels with cloud-flagged NaN pixels excluded per-pixel.}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The OAD score is evaluated along three independent axes: intrinsic lead-time forecastability against baselines (\cref{sec:forecastability}), independent in-situ biological validation at the NEMO mooring (\cref{sec:esp}), and consistency with documented PNW climate events (\cref{sec:climatology}). A short cloud-confound characterization follows (\cref{sec:cloud}).

%-----------
\subsection{Lead-time forecastability vs. baselines}
\label{sec:forecastability}

We compare OAD against a matched-dimensionality PCA baseline and a day-of-year climatology baseline, forecasting per-region anomaly state from the past four days on the 2019+ held-out period. \cref{tab:leadtime} reports results for SW Washington (the highest-amplitude region); \cref{fig:forecastability} shows the cross-region picture at the 7-day horizon.

At the operationally honest lead of 7 days, OAD is the \emph{only} method with positive $R^2$ in every one of the five PNW regions (range 0.10--0.26), while PCA collapses to $\approx 0$ in every region and day-of-year climatology actively anti-predicts. This is evidence that the unsupervised masked-autoencoder learns ocean-state structure beyond what linear or seasonal-decomposition methods can recover.

\subsection{In-situ validation against ESP/ChaBa}
\label{sec:esp}

The cleanest test of whether OAD captures real biology---rather than an idiosyncratic artifact of the unsupervised training objective---is to compare it against independent in-situ measurements at the same time and place. We use ESP-derived \textit{Pn} cell density and particulate domoic acid (pDA) from the 2016--2018 ChaBa deployments at the NEMO mooring (47.97\textdegree\,N, 124.97\textdegree\,W) \cite{Moore2021ESP}. Pearson correlations with 95\% non-parametric bootstrap CIs (2{,}000 resamples, seed = 42) appear in \cref{tab:esp}.

The OAD score correlates significantly with both biological targets at the source region. The strongest \textit{Pn}-cell correlation appears in Olympic Coast (which contains NEMO; $p = 3{\times}10^{-5}$); the strongest pDA correlation appears in SW Washington (the downwind transport corridor; $p = 1{\times}10^{-3}$). Both bootstrap 95\% CIs exclude zero. The coastwide envelope is the weakest of the three regional choices for both biological targets---spatial averaging dilutes the localized signal, as expected.

The per-day scatter (\cref{fig:esp-scatter}) makes the correlation structure visually concrete, and the time-series overlay (\cref{fig:esp-ts}) shows that OAD score peaks and ESP measurement peaks co-evolve during each of the five 2016--2018 deployment windows. Notably, the 2017 spring deployment captured a strong upwelling-driven bloom; OAD detected it both before and during the ESP-measured \textit{Pn} spike, suggesting potential leading-indicator value for in-situ sampling.

%-----------
\subsection{Agreement with documented PNW climatology}
\label{sec:climatology}

The unsupervised score also recovers well-established PNW coastal ocean patterns without ever having been told about specific events. Three patterns emerge.

\noindent\textbf{Seasonal cycle.} The score rises sharply in late spring (April--May), peaks in mid-summer, and declines through autumn across all five regions---matching canonical PNW \textit{Pn} bloom phenology \cite{Trainer2002,Trainer2009,MacCready2021}. Amplitude is largest in SW Washington (Columbia River plume $\times$ shelf upwelling) and Olympic Coast (Juan de Fuca eddy retains nutrient-rich water \cite{Hickey2010}).

\noindent\textbf{Inter-annual variability and the 2014--16 marine heatwave.} Years with documented major HAB events show elevated annual-mean OAD scores. The most striking case is the 2014--16 Pacific marine heatwave (``the Blob'' \cite{Bond2015}), which drove the largest \textit{P.\ australis} bloom on record from southern California through Alaska \cite{McCabe2016,McCabe2023}. The OAD score is clearly elevated across the entire 2014--16 window despite no temporal anomaly labels being seen during training. The 2019 bloom is similarly visible. Quiescent years (e.g.\ 2010, 2012) show suppressed scores.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-region coherence with heterogeneity.} Regional scores correlate at $> 0.5$ on monthly means---consistent with PDO-modulated basin-scale upwelling and ENSO teleconnections \cite{Jacox2018}---but each region preserves its own high-frequency signal from local eddies, plumes, and wind events. This dichotomy is exactly the structure a representation faithful to PNW coastal dynamics should produce.

%-----------
\subsection{Cloud-cover confound}
\label{sec:cloud}

Per-region Pearson correlation between the OAD score and the in-region valid-pixel fraction is moderate: SW Washington shows $r \approx +0.44$ ($\sim$19\% of variance covaries with cloud cover) and Olympic Coast shows $r \approx +0.49$ ($\sim$24\%). The mae070 variant is 4--10 percentage points higher in absolute value, which is why we selected mae050 as the reporting checkpoint. Downstream applications should carry the in-region valid-pixel fraction as a parallel feature so consuming models can learn to discount cloudy weeks.

%================================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

OAD has three independent lines of supporting evidence: intrinsic lead-time forecastability that outperforms PCA and climatology baselines (\cref{sec:forecastability}), significant correlation with in-situ ESP measurements of both \textit{Pn} cells and pDA at the NEMO source (\cref{sec:esp}), and unsupervised recovery of documented inter-annual climate events (\cref{sec:climatology}). Together these establish that the masked-autoencoder learns ocean-state structure that is both intrinsically forecastable beyond linear baselines and biologically meaningful at the source.

The boundary of the representation's predictive reach is the offshore-to-shore propagation: whether the source-region signal predicts beach-level shellfish toxicity depends on wind-driven onshore transport, species-specific toxicity, and shellfish bioaccumulation kinetics, all of which add noise. OAD does not address that propagation; only the offshore validation is reported here.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} (1)~MODIS Aqua sensor sunset will require recalibration to PACE \cite{Werdell2019}; (2)~the 2009--2011 MODIS coverage gap leaves wide uncertainty in some regions; (3)~the cloud-confound is partial ($\sim$19--24\% variance); (4)~in-situ validation uses only one mooring (NEMO) with 76--90 daily samples; (5)~regions are hand-defined---learning regions via clustering on the latent is a natural extension.

%================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced OAD, an unsupervised 3D convolutional masked-autoencoder trained on 22 years of MODIS Aqua imagery over the U.S.\ Pacific Northwest coast, producing a per-region daily anomaly index from per-pixel reconstruction error. The representation outperforms PCA and climatology baselines at multi-day lead times, correlates significantly with independent in-situ ESP measurements of both \textit{Pseudo-nitzschia} cell density ($r{=}{+}0.46$, $p{=}3{\times}10^{-5}$) and particulate domoic acid ($r{=}{+}0.33$, $p{=}1{\times}10^{-3}$) at the NEMO mooring, and recovers documented PNW climate events (2014--16 marine heatwave, 2019 bloom) without supervision. Code and trained checkpoints are available at \url{https://github.com/ansoncchen/DATect-Forecasting-Domoic-Acid} under \texttt{ocean anomaly detection/}.
